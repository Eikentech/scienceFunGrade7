Level 1 · Heat Detective · 8 minutes

Follow the energy.

A hot drink cools while ice melts. Both happen because energy transfers between things at different temperatures.

Temperature is a reading.

Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is. We often measure it in degrees Celsius (°C). Warmer particles generally move or vibrate more energetically.

Heat is energy on the move.

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. It flows naturally from higher to lower temperature. We measure heat in joules (J).

Level 2 · Heat Explorer · 10 minutes

Three ways to travel.

One kitchen can show all three methods. Tap each one to explore.

Conductors pass it along.

Most metals transfer heat easily. A metal pan transfers energy to food; a spoon handle warms even when the handle never touches the soup.

Insulators slow it down.

Wood, foam, and trapped air slow heat transfer. Wool traps air and helps retain warmth from your body. It does not create heat.

Say it precisely: Warm air often rises because it is less dense than the cooler air around it. Heat itself can transfer in any direction.

Melting takes energy.

Ice absorbs energy as it melts. Pure ice at its melting point can absorb energy without getting warmer while it changes state.

Insulation around ice slows energy entering from warmer surroundings, so it slows melting.

Evaporation helps us cool.

Water can evaporate below its boiling point. Evaporating sweat takes energy from your skin. Moving air helps evaporation, which is one reason a fan can help you feel cooler.

Spreading wet clothes exposes more wet surface to air and usually helps them dry faster.

Bonus: why do bridges have expansion joints?

Most materials expand when heated. Expansion joints allow movement as temperatures change. Particles generally move farther apart; the particles themselves do not grow.

Reveal a model explanation

A blanket slows energy transfer. Around soup, it slows energy leaving the warmer soup. Around ice, it slows energy entering from the warmer surroundings.
